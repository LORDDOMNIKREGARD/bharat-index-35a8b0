02 —

India's Borders, Neighbours & Seas

Name all countries that share a land border with India.
Frequently AskedBorders
▾
Answer

India shares land borders with 7 countries: Pakistan (northwest), China (north and northeast), Nepal (north), Bhutan (northeast), Bangladesh (east), Myanmar (east), and Afghanistan (a small stretch through Pakistan-occupied Kashmir, which India claims).

Water borders: India has a maritime boundary with Sri Lanka (Palk Strait) and Maldives (Indian Ocean). The Andaman & Nicobar Islands sit close to Indonesia, Thailand, and Myanmar across the sea.

India Application

Each border relationship has strategic weight — China and Pakistan are adversarial; Nepal and Bhutan are buffer states; Bangladesh is a key trade partner; Myanmar is the gateway to Southeast Asia. For management: the China border affects supply chains (electronics, APIs), while Pakistan relations impact trade routes to Central Asia.

Which northeastern states of India border which countries?
NE IndiaHot Topic
▾
Answer

Arunachal Pradesh — China (disputed McMahon Line) and Bhutan and Myanmar. Sikkim — China (Tibet) and Nepal and Bhutan. Assam — Bhutan (north). Meghalaya — Bangladesh. Mizoram — Bangladesh and Myanmar. Manipur & Nagaland — Myanmar. Tripura — Bangladesh (on three sides).

Meghalaya borders only Bangladesh — a commonly asked IIM question (from an IIM Shillong transcript).

India Application

The Look East / Act East policy makes NE India critical for connectivity to ASEAN. The Kaladan Multimodal Project (India–Myanmar) and India–Bangladesh trade corridors pass through these states, making them economically strategic beyond their border roles.

Which water bodies surround India? What is a peninsula?
Basic Geography
▾
Answer

India is surrounded by the Arabian Sea (west), the Bay of Bengal (east), and the Indian Ocean (south). A peninsula is a landmass that is surrounded by water on three sides but connected to a larger landmass on one side — India's southern half (Deccan Peninsula) fits this definition perfectly.

The Lakshadweep islands lie in the Arabian Sea; Andaman & Nicobar Islands lie in the Bay of Bengal near the Andaman Sea.

India Application

India's peninsular geography gives it a massive 7,500 km coastline with 12 major ports (Mumbai, JNPT, Chennai, Visakhapatnam, Kochi, etc.). This drives maritime trade — about 70% of India's trade by value moves by sea. The Indian Ocean is India's strategic backyard in great-power competition with China's "String of Pearls" strategy.

Does Afghanistan border India? Explain.
Trick Question
▾
Answer

Yes — technically. The Wakhan Corridor, a narrow strip of Afghan territory, meets the Indian-claimed region of Jammu & Kashmir. However, this area is currently part of Pakistan-Occupied Kashmir (PoK). India claims the entire state of J&K, so by India's constitutional claim, Afghanistan does border India. Pakistan controls the physical land separating the two countries currently.

A safe, nuanced answer: "Sir, Afghanistan borders the region that India constitutionally claims as its own — the area currently under Pakistani control. So yes, if India's claim is accepted, they share a very short border through the Wakhan Corridor."

India Application

If India ever had land access to Afghanistan through a peaceful PoK, it would open a direct overland route to Central Asia — transforming India's trade with Iran, the 'Stans, and Russia without dependence on Pakistan. The Chabahar Port in Iran is partly India's workaround for this blockage.

03 —

Where is Egypt? Significance of the Middle East

Where is Egypt? Why is it geopolitically significant?
Key CountryAfrica + ME
▾
Answer

Egypt is located in northeast Africa, bordering the Mediterranean Sea (north), Red Sea (east), Libya (west), Sudan (south), and Israel/Gaza (northeast). The Sinai Peninsula makes Egypt a transcontinental nation — it bridges Africa and Asia.

Geopolitical significance: (1) Suez Canal — Egypt controls the Suez Canal, through which roughly 12–15% of global trade passes; closure would force ships around Africa (adding 10–14 days). (2) Arab World — Largest Arab nation by population (~105 million); its political direction shapes Arab sentiment. (3) Israel–Palestine — Egypt is a key mediator and controls the Rafah crossing into Gaza. (4) BRICS / G20 membership — Egypt joined BRICS in 2024.

India Application

Any disruption in the Suez Canal directly raises India's shipping costs. During the 2021 Ever Given blockage, India's trade was impacted. Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping in 2024 forced Indian exporters to reroute around Africa, increasing freight costs by 30–40%. Egypt is also a rising market for Indian pharmaceuticals, IT services, and machinery exports. India–Egypt bilateral trade crossed $7 billion in FY24.

Interview Tip When asked about Egypt, lead with Suez Canal. That's the hook interviewers usually want you to reach for.
What is the significance of the Middle East for the world and for India?
Frequently AskedEnergy
▾
Answer

The Middle East (loosely: Arabian Peninsula + Levant + Iran + Iraq + Egypt) matters for five reasons:

1. Energy: Houses roughly 48% of the world's proven oil reserves and 40% of natural gas. OPEC+ decisions here set global energy prices.

2. Trade chokepoints: The Strait of Hormuz (between Iran and Oman) carries ~20% of global oil; the Suez Canal (Egypt) carries ~12% of global trade; the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait (Yemen/Djibouti) is another crucial link.

3. Three world religions — Islam, Christianity, Judaism — have holy sites in the region, giving it permanent civilisational significance.

4. Great-power competition — The US, Russia, China, and regional powers (Saudi Arabia, Iran, Turkey, Israel) all have deep stakes here.

5. Diaspora remittances — Millions of workers from South/Southeast Asia work in Gulf states and send money home.

India Application

Energy dependency: India imports about 80% of its crude oil; the Gulf supplies ~60% of that. A $10 rise in Brent crude costs India roughly $15 billion more per year. Diaspora: ~8.9 million Indians live in the Gulf (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain). They remitted ~$40 billion to India in 2023, making this the largest single remittance corridor. Diplomatic balancing: India maintains strong ties with both Arab Gulf states AND Israel, while also preserving relations with Iran (Chabahar Port). This "strategic autonomy" approach is a key pillar of Indian foreign policy.

What is the Strait of Hormuz? Why does India care about it?
Chokepoint
▾
Answer

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow sea passage (~33 km wide at its narrowest) between Iran and the Sultanate of Oman. It connects the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and then to the Arabian Sea. About 20% of global oil trade (roughly 20 million barrels per day) passes through it — making it the world's most important oil chokepoint.

If Iran closes the Strait (a threat it has made during US sanctions), it would cause an immediate global oil supply shock, spiking prices and triggering economic turmoil.

India Application

A closure of Hormuz would cut off India from its primary oil suppliers (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq, Kuwait). India's strategic oil reserves (stored in underground caverns in Vishakhapatnam, Mangalore, and Padur) cover only about 9–10 days of consumption — meaning even a brief closure would have severe consequences. This is why India invests in energy diversification (Russia, Africa, USA) and in domestic renewable energy.

Explain the Israel–Iran/Gaza conflict and India's position.
Hot TopicAsked in IIM Ranchi, Amritsar, Indore
▾
Answer

Background: The Israel–Palestine conflict traces back to 1947–48 with the creation of Israel and displacement of Palestinian Arabs. Gaza is a Palestinian territory governed by Hamas (designated a terrorist organisation by the US, EU, Israel). The West Bank is governed by the Palestinian Authority.

October 7, 2023 escalation: Hamas launched a mass surprise attack on southern Israel, killing ~1,200 Israelis. Israel responded with a large-scale military offensive in Gaza, causing massive civilian casualties. The conflict drew in Iran (via Hezbollah in Lebanon, Houthis in Yemen) and triggered US military involvement in the Red Sea.

Iran angle: Iran views Israel's existence as illegitimate; it funds and arms Hamas and Hezbollah. Israel and Iran exchanged direct strikes for the first time in April–May 2024, significantly escalating tensions.

India's Position & Application

Diplomatically: India has historically supported Palestinian statehood (consistent with NAM principles) while simultaneously building a strong strategic partnership with Israel (defence, technology, agriculture). In the UNGA vote in October 2023, India abstained on a ceasefire resolution — threading the needle between both relationships. Economically: The conflict disrupted Red Sea shipping (Houthi attacks), inflating India's import costs. India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) — announced at G20 New Delhi — has been stalled by the conflict. Also, Israel is India's 3rd largest defence supplier.

04 —

Global Organisations & India's Role

What is BRICS? Who are its members and what does India gain from it?
Frequently Asked
▾
Answer

BRICS originally stood for Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa — a grouping of major emerging economies coined by Goldman Sachs economist Jim O'Neill in 2001. In 2024, BRICS expanded (BRICS+) to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE. Saudi Arabia was invited but hasn't formally joined.

Key features: collective GDP represents ~26% of global output (PPP terms), ~40% of world population. The grouping advocates for a multipolar world order and reform of international financial institutions (IMF, World Bank). The New Development Bank (NDB), headquartered in Shanghai, is BRICS's multilateral development bank.

Recent agenda: de-dollarisation — reducing dependence on the US dollar in international trade. India has been cautious here, balancing its relationship with the US.

India Application

India uses BRICS as a platform to advocate for greater voice in global governance (UNSC permanent seat). The NDB has funded several Indian infrastructure projects. However, India faces a fundamental tension within BRICS: China is both its largest trading partner and its primary strategic adversary. Post-Galwan (2020 border clash), India's BRICS participation has been measured. India also doesn't fully back de-dollarisation, fearing it could destabilise its own dollar-denominated reserves and trade.

What is SAARC? Why is it ineffective?
South AsiaIIM Amritsar, Indore
▾
Answer

SAARC (South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation) was founded in 1985 and has 8 members: India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, Afghanistan, and Maldives. Secretariat is in Kathmandu.

SAARC meetings require consensus — Pakistan's obstruction (especially after India's Uri/Pulwama attacks) has paralysed the forum. The last SAARC Summit was held in 2014 (in Kathmandu). India has not attended a summit since 2016. As a result, SAARC is largely defunct in practice.

India's workaround: BIMSTEC (Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation) — includes India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Nepal, Bhutan. No Pakistan, so it functions better.

India Application

South Asia is the least economically integrated region in the world — intra-regional trade is only ~5% of total trade (compare to ASEAN at ~25%). This is almost entirely due to India–Pakistan tensions. If normalised, the India–Pakistan trade relationship alone could be worth $37 billion annually (currently ~$2 billion via third countries). For India, a functional SAARC would mean regional supply chains, shared infrastructure, and reduced costs — a $5 trillion economy aspiration depends partly on regional integration.

What is the SCO (Shanghai Cooperation Organisation)?
GeopoliticsIIM Indore transcript
▾
Answer

The SCO is a Eurasian political, economic, and security organisation founded in 2001 in Shanghai. Members: China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, India (joined 2017), Pakistan (joined 2017), Iran (joined 2023), Belarus (joined 2024). Secretariat in Beijing.

Focus areas: counter-terrorism, border security, economic cooperation. It is often described as a China-Russia led counterweight to Western-dominated institutions (NATO, G7).

India Application

India's SCO membership gives it a seat at the table with Central Asian states — crucial for energy access and the International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC). However, India has boycotted some SCO meetings hosted by Pakistan. In June 2023, India hosted the SCO summit virtually (as chair). The awkward reality: India shares the SCO with both Pakistan and China, its two main security concerns.

Difference between G7 and G20. Is India in G7?
Direct GK
▾
Answer

G7 (Group of Seven): USA, UK, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan — plus the European Union as a non-enumerated member. These are the world's 7 largest advanced (Western/democratic) economies. India is NOT in G7.

G20 (Group of Twenty): G7 + 13 more — Argentina, Australia, Brazil, China, India, Indonesia, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Turkey + EU. India IS a founding G20 member. India hosted the G20 in New Delhi in September 2023 — a landmark event where the African Union was admitted as a permanent member.

India Application

India's G20 presidency (Dec 2022 – Nov 2023) was significant: India pushed for the Global South's voice, secured debt relief frameworks for developing nations, and had the African Union admitted. Prime Minister Modi coined the theme "Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam" (One Earth, One Family, One Future). This marked India's arrival as a major shaper of the global order — not just a participant.

Rivers, Mountains & Physical Geography

What is the longest river of India? Name major rivers and their origins.
Asked in IIM Sirmaur transcript
▾
Answer

The Ganga is India's longest river entirely within India (~2,525 km). The Indus is longer overall (~3,200 km) but most of it flows through Pakistan. The Godavari is the longest Peninsular river (~1,465 km), originating in Nashik, Maharashtra, flowing east to the Bay of Bengal.

Key rivers and origins: Ganga — Gangotri Glacier (Uttarakhand). Brahmaputra — Angsi Glacier (Tibet, enters India through Arunachal Pradesh). Yamuna — Yamunotri Glacier (Uttarakhand). Narmada — Amarkantak Plateau (MP), flows WEST into Arabian Sea. Tapti — Betul (MP), flows west. Krishna — Western Ghats (Maharashtra), flows east. Kaveri — Kodagu (Karnataka), flows east. Mahanadi — Chhattisgarh, flows east.

India Application

Rivers shape India's agriculture (Gangetic plain feeds 400 million+), power (hydroelectric), and trade (inland waterways). Water-sharing treaties with Pakistan (Indus Waters Treaty, 1960) and Bangladesh (Teesta River sharing dispute) are geopolitical issues. India threatened to renegotiate the Indus Waters Treaty after the Pahalgam attack in 2025, signalling water as a foreign policy tool.

Which mountain range divides northern and southern India?
Asked in IIM Ranchi transcript
▾
Answer

The Vindhya Range (and associated Satpura range) broadly divides the Indo-Gangetic Plain (north) from the Deccan Plateau (south). The Vindhyas run east-west through Madhya Pradesh. The Satpura Range runs parallel to the south. Together they form the watershed between rivers flowing north (Ganga basin) and south/west (Narmada, Tapti).

Note: The Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats run north-south along India's two coasts and divide coastal areas from the Deccan interior.

Highest peak in India: Kangchenjunga (8,586 m, Sikkim). K2 (8,611 m, PoK) is higher but India claims it — a nuanced answer earns respect in interviews.

India Application

The Vindhya-Satpura belt is rich in mineral resources (coal, iron ore, manganese). The Western Ghats are a UNESCO World Heritage Site — a biodiversity hotspot. Destruction of these ranges for mining has been a key environmental debate in India, making it relevant to business sustainability discussions.

Trade, Tariffs & Geo-economics

What are tariffs? Why is the US imposing tariffs (Trump's policy)? How does it affect India?
Highly RecurrentAsked in 15+ transcripts
▾
Answer

Tariffs are taxes levied by a government on imported goods. They make foreign products more expensive, incentivising domestic buying. Types: ad valorem (% of value), specific (fixed amount per unit), compound, retaliatory.

Trump's tariff policy (2025): In his second term, Trump imposed sweeping tariffs — including a 26% tariff on Indian exports to the US under "reciprocal tariffs," justified by arguing that India's own tariffs on US goods are high. Trump also imposed a 145% tariff on China. The stated goal: "Make America Great Again" — bring manufacturing back to the US, reduce trade deficits, protect American workers. The "One Big Beautiful Bill" (domestic companion legislation) extended tax cuts and spending priorities.

India Application

Direct impact: India exports ~$80 billion to the US annually (IT services, pharmaceuticals, gems/jewellery, textiles). A 26% tariff makes these more expensive for American buyers. IT services (India's top export) are largely exempt (services vs goods), but goods exporters are hit. Indirect opportunity: With China tariffed at 145%, some manufacturing orders may shift from China to India (textiles, electronics, chemicals). India is in active trade negotiations with the US to reduce tariffs. The outcome shapes India's export-led growth ambitions.

What is India's geopolitical stance? What is strategic autonomy?
Asked in IIM Sirmaur transcript
▾
Answer

India's foreign policy is guided by strategic autonomy — the principle of not permanently aligning with any single power bloc, making independent decisions based on national interest. Rooted in Nehru's Non-Aligned Movement (NAM), it has evolved under Modi into what is sometimes called "multi-alignment" — building partnerships with the US, Russia, EU, China, and the Gulf simultaneously.

Examples: India buys Russian oil while also being a QUAD member with the US, Japan, and Australia. India abstains on Russia-Ukraine UNGA votes. India is in the SCO (with China and Pakistan) while also participating in the Indo-Pacific strategy countering China.

India Application

Strategic autonomy is both a strength and a source of friction with allies. Post-Ukraine, the West pressured India to cut Russian oil imports; India refused, buying discounted Russian crude that reduced its import bill by ~$12 billion and used the savings to fund domestic development. For business: this means India is a complex market where political risk has multiple dimensions — not simply "US-allied" or "China-aligned."

What is the Chabahar Port and why is it strategically important for India?
Trade Route
▾
Answer

Chabahar Port is a deep-water port located in Sistan-Baluchestan province of Iran, on the Gulf of Oman (not the Persian Gulf). India has invested in and is developing the Shahid Beheshti terminal at Chabahar.

It is located just outside the Strait of Hormuz, giving it strategic maritime value. A road/rail network from Chabahar through Iran connects to Afghanistan and onwards to Central Asia.

India Application

Bypass Pakistan: India has no overland access to Afghanistan or Central Asia because Pakistan doesn't allow Indian transit. Chabahar provides an alternative — goods can go from India by ship to Chabahar, then by road through Iran to Kabul, bypassing Pakistan entirely. Counter China: China is building the Gwadar Port in Pakistan (60 km from Chabahar) as part of CPEC. Chabahar is India's counterplay. Central Asia trade: India aims to access the $500 billion Central Asian market. In 2024, India signed a 10-year agreement to operate Chabahar, despite US sanction risks on Iran.

Active Conflicts & Flashpoints

Explain the Russia–Ukraine war. India's position?
Major Conflict
▾
Answer

Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine on February 24, 2022, following years of tensions over NATO expansion and the status of eastern Ukrainian regions (Donbas). Russia annexed Crimea in 2014 and recognised the breakaway Donetsk and Luhansk republics in 2022 before invading.

As of 2025, the war is ongoing. Ukraine has received massive Western military and financial support. Russia faces Western sanctions. Both sides have suffered significant casualties. Peace negotiations have been periodically discussed but without conclusion.

Key implication: Global food prices rose sharply (Ukraine and Russia together supply ~30% of world's wheat); energy prices surged in Europe; global supply chains were disrupted.

India Application

India has maintained a position of "dialogue and diplomacy." It has not condemned Russia at the UNSC or UNGA, abstaining on most votes — protecting its access to discounted Russian oil (~$35–40/barrel vs market price ~$80+) and Russian defence equipment (~60% of India's military hardware is Russian-origin). India is also mediating — PM Modi visited both Kyiv (August 2024) and Moscow (July 2024), positioning India as a potential peacemaker. For business: Russia–Ukraine disrupted fertiliser supply (Russia is a major urea exporter), affecting Indian farmers; defence imports became critical policy focus.

What happened in Pahalgam (2025)? What is Operation Sindoor?
2025 News — HotAsked in IIM Ranchi transcripts
▾
Answer

In April 2025, a terrorist attack in Pahalgam (Baisaran Valley), Jammu & Kashmir killed 26 tourists, making it one of the deadliest attacks on civilians in J&K in years. Terrorists targeted tourists in a meadow area. The attack was attributed to Pakistan-based groups by Indian intelligence.

Operation Sindoor (May 7, 2025): India launched precision military strikes on terrorist infrastructure in Pakistan and Pakistan-occupied Kashmir, targeting 9 sites associated with groups like Lashkar-e-Taiba and Jaish-e-Mohammed. India described it as a "measured, non-escalatory" response targeting only terrorists, not Pakistani military assets. Pakistan responded with limited counter-fire. A ceasefire was announced on May 10, 2025, following international mediation.

India Application

Operation Sindoor marked a significant shift in India's deterrence doctrine — demonstrating willingness to conduct cross-border strikes in response to terrorism. Economic implications: India suspended the Indus Waters Treaty after the attack, escalating water as a diplomatic weapon. Trade through Wagah border was halted. Insurance premiums on Pakistan-adjacent regions rose. The operation strengthened domestic political consensus but added risk premia to India–Pakistan economic relations. Markets initially dipped but recovered quickly, showing investor confidence in India's stability.

What do you know about the Northeast and its culture?
IIM ShillongVery Frequently Asked
▾
Answer

The Northeast is home to over 200 tribal communities speaking hundreds of dialects. Key cultural highlights: Bihu (Assam's harvest festival), Hornbill Festival (Nagaland — a showcase of all Naga tribes), Sangai Festival (Manipur). Meghalaya is home to the Khasi and Garo tribes with matrilineal societies (property passes through women). Mizoram has over 90% literacy, one of India's highest.

The region is connected to the rest of India through a narrow land corridor in West Bengal known as the "Chicken's Neck" or Siliguri Corridor — a 22 km wide strip. It is India's most strategically vulnerable geographic point.

India Application

The Northeast is India's gateway to ASEAN (the Act East Policy). Yet it remains underdeveloped — per capita income in most NE states is below the national average. Development challenges: insurgencies (gradually declining), poor connectivity, limited industry. India has invested heavily in road and rail connectivity (including the Bogibeel Bridge — India's longest rail-road bridge, in Assam). For IIM Shillong students: the institute itself is located in one of the 7 Sisters and serves as a management hub for the region.

What is the Chicken's Neck? Why is it strategically critical?
Strategic Geography
▾
Answer

The Siliguri Corridor (Chicken's Neck) is a narrow strip of land (~22–40 km wide) in West Bengal that connects mainland India to the 7 northeastern states. It is flanked by Nepal (north), Bangladesh (south), and Bhutan (northeast).

If this corridor were cut off — in a military conflict with China/Nepal, or through political instability — the entire Northeast would be isolated from India. China's presence in Chumbi Valley (Tibet) puts Chinese forces within striking distance of the corridor. This is why India has heavily invested in the Bogibeel Bridge, Dhola-Sadiya Bridge, and multiple rail links to provide redundant connectivity.

India Application

The Chicken's Neck vulnerability is the primary reason India resists any Sino–Nepalese or Sino–Bhutanese territorial moves. It also explains why India maintains deep engagement with Bangladesh (ensuring political stability there prevents the southern flank of the corridor from becoming hostile). For business: this vulnerability means insurers and logistics firms charge higher risk premiums for NE India operations, limiting investment inflows.
