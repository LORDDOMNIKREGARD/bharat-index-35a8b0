§4 Algebraic & Polynomial Functions

Quadraticy = ax²+bx+c
Parabola. Opens up if a>0, down if a<0. Vertex at x=−b/2a.
Discriminant Δ=b²−4ac: Δ>0→2 roots, Δ=0→1 root, Δ<0→no real roots.
vertex=turning pointasked: every IIM!
roots: x=(−b±√Δ)/2a  ·  vertex: (−b/2a, −Δ/4a)
Cubicy = x³
Odd function. Inflection at origin. Steeper growth than x².
d/dx=3x²≥0 always → always non-decreasing.
oddinflection at (0,0)asked: Indore, Ranchi
d/dx(x³)=3x²  ·  ∫x³dx=x⁴/4+C
Lineary = mx + c
m=slope (dy/dx). c=y-intercept. x-intercept=−c/m.
Parallel lines: same slope. Perpendicular: m₁·m₂=−1.
slope = dy/dxasked: every IIM!
slope of 2x+5y=10 → y=−(2/5)x+2, m=−2/5
Modulus |x|y = |x|
V-shaped. Even function. Continuous everywhere.
NOT differentiable at x=0 (sharp corner — two tangents).
evennot differentiable at 0asked: Indore, Amritsar
|x|=x if x≥0; =−x if x<0  ·  example: cont. but not diff.
y=1/xRectangular Hyperbola

oddasymptotes: x=0, y=0asked: Indore Mumbai

§8 Statistics & Probability Graphs

Normal DistributionBell Curve, μ=0, σ=1
Symmetric about mean μ. Inflection points at μ±σ.
Empirical rule: 68%→±σ, 95%→±2σ, 99.7%→±3σ
Total area under curve = 1. Mean=Median=Mode at centre.
symmetricarea=1asked: Indore, Ranchi, Shillong!
f(x) = (1/σ√2π)·e^[−½((x−μ)/σ)²]
Skewed Distributionsleft vs right skew
Right-skewed (positive): Tail right. Mean > Median > Mode.
Left-skewed (negative): Tail left. Mean < Median < Mode.
If mean=50 & median=45 → positive/right skew (mean pulled right).
asked: Amritsar (mean=50, median=45)
Pearson's skewness = 3(mean−median)/σ
Standard Deviationσ on the bell curve
σ=spread of data. Low σ → tall narrow bell. High σ → wide short bell.
Variance = σ². SD is in same units as data.
Why SD over variance? → SD is in same units as the data.
asked: Amritsar, Ranchi, Kozhikode, Shillong!
σ=√[Σ(xᵢ−μ)²/n]  ·  Variance=σ²=Σ(xᵢ−μ)²/n
Mean, Median, Modeon a distribution curve
Mode: Peak (most frequent). Median: Halves the area. Mean: Centre of gravity.
Normal: M=M=M at centre. Mean most affected by outliers.
Skewed right: Mode < Median < Mean.
asked: every single IIM!
Normal: mean=median=mode  ·  Right skew: mode<median<mean

§9 Economics Graphs

Demand CurveP = f(Q), downward
Downward sloping: higher price → lower quantity demanded.
P on Y-axis, Q on X-axis. Shifts on income/preference change.
P on Y, Q on Xasked: every IIM!
Elasticity = (%ΔQ)/(%ΔP)  ·  negative for normal goods
Consumer & Producer Surplusareas above/below P*
CS (blue): area between demand curve and equilibrium price.
PS (red): area between supply curve and equilibrium price.
Both are integrals in disguise.
asked: Ranchi, Amritsar, Shillong, Sirmaur
CS = ∫₀^q* D(q)dq − P*q*  ·  PS = P*q* − ∫₀^q* S(q)dq
MU & TU (LDMU)Diminishing Marginal Utility
TU: rises then falls. MU: falls from start.
Key: TU is maximum when MU = 0 (derivative=0).
Law of DMU: MU decreases with more consumption.
asked: Ranchi, Amritsar, Shillong, Sirmaur!
MU = d(TU)/dQ  ·  TU max ↔ MU=0 (same logic as maxima)
Indifference CurvesIC + Budget Line
ICs: convex to origin, downward sloping, never intersect.
Consumer equilibrium: where IC is tangent to budget line.
MRS = Px/Py at optimum.
asked: Shillong, Ranchi, Amritsar, Kozhikode
MRS = MUx/MUy = Px/Py at equilibrium (tangency)
MC & AC Curvescost curves
Both U-shaped. MC: rises steeply. AC: rises more gradually.
MC crosses AC at AC's minimum. Always.
MC < AC → AC falling. MC > AC → AC rising.
asked: Ranchi Bangalore, Shillong
MC = d(TC)/dQ  ·  MC crosses AC at AC minimum
TP & MP Curvesproduction function
TP: rises then falls. MP: peaks before TP, then hits zero.
Key: TP is maximum when MP = 0
MP negative → TP falling (over-employment).
asked: Ranchi
MP = d(TP)/dL  ·  TP max ↔ MP=0 (exact same as TU/MU!)
