US Tariffs & Trade War
IIM Shillong IIM Amritsar IIM Indore
What are tariffs? What are reciprocal tariffs? How do they affect India?
Structured Answer
Tariffs are taxes imposed by a government on imported goods, raising the cost of foreign products to protect domestic industries and generate revenue. Reciprocal tariffs are matching levies — when Country A imposes duties on Country B's goods, Country B retaliates with equivalent tariffs on A's exports.

US Context (2025): Under President Trump's administration, the US imposed a 26% tariff on Indian exports as part of its broad reciprocal tariff policy, alongside higher tariffs on China. This was the "One Big Beautiful Bill" approach to trade protectionism, framing tariffs as tools to make America's trade balance favorable.

Impact on India: Indian export sectors like textiles, pharmaceuticals, IT hardware, and gems face higher barriers in the US market. However, India may benefit from trade diversion — buyers seeking alternatives to Chinese goods. The rupee may face depreciation pressure. India has begun bilateral trade negotiations with the US for exemptions.
IIM Shillong IIM Amritsar IIM Indore
What did Trump want to achieve through his tariffs? What is the "One Big Beautiful Bill"?
Structured Answer
Trump's tariff policy aimed to reduce the US trade deficit, revive domestic manufacturing ("reshoring"), use tariffs as leverage in international negotiations, and generate revenue to fund tax cuts. The "One Big Beautiful Bill" is a broad legislative package combining tax cuts, spending reductions, and protectionist trade measures. It represents a consolidation of Trump's economic agenda — lowering domestic taxes while raising the cost of foreign competition through tariffs.
IIM Amritsar IIM Indore
Impact of US tariffs on Indian consumers and businesses?
Structured Answer
For Indian exporters: Higher entry barriers in the US market reduce competitiveness, especially for IT, pharma, textiles, and gems. For Indian consumers: US tariffs on China may redirect Chinese goods to India at cheaper prices, benefiting consumers but hurting domestic Indian producers. For Indian businesses importing US goods: If India retaliates, costs for US technology and agricultural imports rise. The net effect depends on the bilateral trade deal outcome.
IIM Amritsar
How would India be affected if the Strait of Hormuz were blocked?
Structured Answer
The Strait of Hormuz is a critical chokepoint through which nearly 20% of the world's oil transits. India imports roughly 85% of its crude oil, a significant portion transiting this strait.

If blocked: (1) Global oil prices would spike sharply, raising India's import bill and worsening its current account deficit. (2) Inflation would accelerate due to higher fuel and transport costs. (3) India might benefit from increased demand for Russian oil via alternative routes. (4) India's strategic oil reserves provide only short-term buffer. The candidate who mentioned "benefit from selling Russian crude oil to other markets" was on the right track regarding market reorientation, but the primary impact remains negative for India as a net importer.
Iran-Israel Conflict
IIM Amritsar IIM Ranchi IIM Indore
What is the Iran-Israel conflict about? How did it start? What missiles are being used?
Structured Answer
The Iran-Israel conflict is a long-standing proxy and direct confrontation rooted in ideological, religious, and strategic rivalry. Iran does not recognize Israel and has historically supported anti-Israel groups (Hezbollah, Hamas). The conflict escalated significantly in April 2024 when Iran launched its first-ever direct missile and drone attack on Israeli territory, followed by Israeli retaliatory strikes on Iran.

Context of 2025 escalation: US military involvement increased, with reports of American strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities using B-2 stealth bombers. The conflict intersects with the Gaza war (Hamas-Israel conflict from October 2023).

Missiles used: Iran deploys Shahab-3, Fateh-110, and Fattah hypersonic missiles. Israel uses the Arrow and Iron Dome defense systems, along with F-35 jets. The US uses B-2 Spirit stealth bombers capable of penetrating air defenses — "stealth" refers to the aircraft's ability to absorb or scatter radar waves, making it nearly invisible to radar detection.
IIM Amritsar IIM Ranchi
Impact of the Iran-Israel conflict on India? What is India's diplomatic stand?
Structured Answer
Economic Impact: Oil price volatility — Iran is a major producer and the Strait of Hormuz runs adjacent to Iranian waters. Higher oil prices increase India's import bill and inflation. Indian diaspora in the Gulf region (over 8 million) faces security risks.

India's Position: India maintains a balanced stance — it has diplomatic ties with both Israel and Iran, buys discounted Russian and Iranian oil, and maintains trade relations with Gulf states that are in the conflict's orbit. India advocates for dialogue and a diplomatic resolution. As a potential Indian diplomat, the correct stance is neutrality while pushing for peace negotiations, prioritizing India's energy security and diaspora safety above alliance-based positions.

BRICS & Multilateral Forums
IIM Shillong IIM Amritsar IIM Indore
What is BRICS? What is the BRICS tariff issue affecting India? What is de-dollarization?
Structured Answer
BRICS (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa) is an intergovernmental bloc of major emerging economies. In 2024-25, it expanded to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE (BRICS+).

BRICS & Tariffs: The US threatened BRICS nations with 100% tariffs if they proceeded with plans to reduce dependence on the US dollar in trade — this is the "BRICS tariff" referenced in interviews.

De-dollarization: The push by BRICS nations to conduct international trade in local currencies or a new BRICS currency, reducing reliance on the US dollar. India has been exploring rupee-based trade, especially with Russia for oil purchases. The agenda reflects broader geopolitical shifts in the global financial order.

The SCO focuses on regional security, counter-terrorism, and economic cooperation. However, India has often faced tensions within it due to China-Pakistan alignment. India reportedly declined to sign certain joint declarations at SCO summits that conflicted with its positions on terrorism or connectivity projects running through Pakistan-occupied Kashmir.

IIM Indore
What is the AfCFTA (African Continental Free Trade Area)?
Structured Answer
AfCFTA is one of the world's largest free trade areas by number of countries, bringing together 54 African Union member states. It came into force in 2019 and aims to create a single continental market for goods and services, eliminate tariffs on 90% of intra-African trade, and boost industrial development. For India, AfCFTA presents opportunities in pharmaceuticals, IT services, and manufacturing investment in Africa, as the continent integrates economically.

Indian Economy & Monetary Policy

RBI, repo rate, inflation, GDP, fiscal policy

IIM Shillong IIM Amritsar IIM Ranchi IIM Indore
What is the current repo rate? Why did the RBI reduce it? What is India's current inflation rate?
Structured Answer
Repo Rate (2025): The RBI reduced the repo rate to 6.00% (as of 2025 cuts), down from 6.50%, in two consecutive 25 basis point cuts. This was the first rate cut in several years.

Why the cut: Retail inflation (CPI) moderated to around 3.6-4% — well within RBI's target band of 4% (±2%). With inflation controlled, the RBI shifted focus to stimulating growth. A lower repo rate reduces the cost of borrowing for banks, which pass this on as cheaper loans to businesses and consumers, stimulating investment and consumption.

RBI's 4% target: RBI targets 4% inflation because mild inflation encourages spending over saving, drives production incentives, and provides buffer against deflation. 0% inflation risks a deflationary spiral where consumers postpone purchases expecting further price falls.

IIM Amritsar IIM Ranchi IIM Indore
What is India's current GDP? Which is higher — India's GDP or GNP? What is per capita income?
Structured Answer
India's GDP (Nominal, 2024-25): Approximately $3.9 trillion, making India the 5th largest economy globally (in nominal terms). In PPP terms, India is the 3rd largest.

GDP vs GNP: GDP = value of all goods and services produced within India's borders. GNP = GDP + income earned by Indians abroad − income earned by foreigners in India. For India, GDP is generally higher than GNP because more foreigners earn in India (remitting profits abroad) than Indians earn from overseas assets.

Per Capita Income: Approximately $2,500-2,700 (nominal). India uses GDP at market prices as its primary measure, calculated by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). India has moved from the "expenditure method" to a comprehensive national accounts framework.
IIM Amritsar IIM Ranchi IIM Indore
What is fiscal deficit? Primary deficit? What is the difference between fiscal and monetary policy?
Structured Answer
Fiscal Deficit = Total Government Expenditure − Total Revenue (excluding borrowings). It represents how much the government borrows to fund its spending. India's fiscal deficit target for FY2025-26 is ~4.5% of GDP.

Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments. It shows the deficit excluding legacy debt burden.

Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts. It indicates the gap in the government's day-to-day operations.

Fiscal Policy = Government's use of taxation and spending to influence the economy (controlled by Ministry of Finance).
Monetary Policy = Central bank's management of money supply and interest rates to control inflation and growth (controlled by RBI in India). They are complementary — loose fiscal + tight monetary is a common combination.

IIM Amritsar IIM Indore
What is stagflation? What is quantitative easing? Name two economies that used it recently.
Structured Answer
Stagflation = Simultaneous high inflation + stagnant economic growth + high unemployment. It's particularly challenging because standard remedies conflict — raising rates to fight inflation worsens growth and unemployment.

Quantitative Easing (QE) = A central bank creates new money digitally and uses it to purchase government bonds and financial assets, injecting liquidity into the economy to lower long-term interest rates and stimulate lending. Recent examples: US Federal Reserve (extensive QE during COVID-19, 2020-21) and European Central Bank (pandemic emergency purchase program, 2020-22).
IIM Amritsar
What is India's current account deficit (CAD)? How is it different from trade deficit?
Structured Answer
Trade Deficit = Imports of goods − Exports of goods. Only covers merchandise trade.

Current Account Deficit (CAD) = (Trade Deficit) + (Services Balance) + (Primary Income) + (Secondary Income/Transfers). CAD is broader — it includes trade in services (where India has a surplus due to IT/software exports), investment income, and remittances. India typically has a trade deficit but a smaller CAD because its services surplus and remittances partially offset the goods deficit.

In FY2024-25, India's CAD moderated to around 0.5-1% of GDP — a manageable level.
IIM Ranchi IIM Indore
Is GDP a good measure of national income? What are its limitations?
Structured Answer
GDP is a useful but incomplete indicator. Limitations: (1) Ignores income distribution — high GDP can coexist with extreme inequality (hence Gini Coefficient is supplementary). (2) Excludes unpaid work — household labor, caregiving, volunteer work. (3) Does not account for environmental degradation or sustainability. (4) Misses the informal/shadow economy, which is significant in India. (5) Does not measure well-being, health, or happiness (alternatives: HDI, GNH).

Alternatives India uses: India computes GDP at market prices using the expenditure method and cross-checks with income and output methods. MoSPI releases advance estimates, revised estimates, and final estimates.

IIM Sirmaur IIM Shillong
What is the language conflict in Bangalore? How does it affect development and companies?
Structured Answer
The Kannada vs Hindi controversy in Bengaluru centres on demands from pro-Kannada groups that Kannada be mandatory in workplaces, signboards, and public life. There is resentment among locals that the city's IT boom has attracted large numbers of Hindi-speaking migrants, diluting Kannada culture.

Impact on development: Linguistic nationalism creates friction with businesses that rely on a pan-Indian workforce. Companies may consider relocating to other cities if language mandates become burdensome. It can deter investment and talent mobility. However, the conflict also reflects legitimate concerns about cultural preservation and equitable economic participation of native residents.

Impact on companies: If firms are compelled to hire only Kannada speakers or face protests, their talent pool shrinks, increasing operational costs and potentially reducing competitiveness.

Mumbai's economic vibrancy depends on its cosmopolitan character; alienating communities would damage it.

Business, Markets & Corporate News

Hindenburg, Adani, stock markets, corporate developments

IIM Sirmaur IIM Amritsar
Why was Hindenburg Research in the news? What is a short seller?
Structured Answer
Hindenburg Research is an American activist short-seller firm that published a damning report on the Adani Group in January 2023, alleging stock manipulation, accounting fraud, and improper use of offshore tax havens across 7 Adani listed companies. The report triggered a massive stock market crash in Adani Group shares (losing ~$100 billion in market cap at peak).

A short seller borrows shares, sells them at current high prices, waits for the price to fall (sometimes by publishing negative research), then buys them back cheaper to return to the lender, profiting from the price difference. Hindenburg's business model is controversial — it profits from the very declines its reports cause.

SEBI investigated and a Supreme Court-appointed committee largely cleared Adani of the most serious charges, though some questions remain. Hindenburg Research shut down in January 2025.

IIM Amritsar IIM Ranchi
What is MSP (Minimum Support Price)? Is it necessary?
Structured Answer
MSP is the price at which the government commits to purchase crops from farmers, providing a floor price safety net. The Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) recommends MSPs for 23 crops.

Arguments FOR MSP: Protects farmers from market volatility and distress selling; ensures food security by incentivizing production; supports rural incomes.

Arguments AGAINST / Limitations: Benefits primarily reach large farmers with surplus; creates market distortions; inflated MSPs contribute to food inflation; government procurement capacity is limited to a few crops (mainly wheat and rice) in certain states. The 2020-21 farm law protests centered on demands for legal guarantee of MSP, which the government eventually rejected.
IIM Shillong
What is Black Money? What are its implications on National Income calculation and policy making?
Structured Answer
Black Money refers to income earned through illegal means or income that is legally earned but concealed from tax authorities to evade taxes. It operates in the shadow/informal/parallel economy.

Implications on National Income: GDP and NI are underestimated because black money transactions are not reported. India's shadow economy is estimated at 20-25% of GDP. Tax revenue is lower than it should be, reducing government capacity for public spending.

Implications on Policy: (1) Monetary policy effectiveness is reduced — RBI's interest rate signals don't reach the informal economy. (2) Fiscal policy is hampered by a narrow tax base. (3) Wealth inequality is understated. (4) Policy interventions like demonetization (2016) were partly aimed at flushing out black money. (5) Income inequality data is distorted.

Science, Technology & Innovation

AI, rare earth elements, internet, ISRO

IIM Shillong IIM Indore
What is the importance of rare earth elements in India today?
Structured Answer
Rare Earth Elements (REEs) are 17 metallic elements (lanthanides + scandium and yttrium) critical for high-tech manufacturing — electric vehicle batteries, wind turbines, smartphones, defense equipment, MRI machines.

India's context: India has the 5th largest rare earth reserves globally, concentrated in states like Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Odisha. However, India has been dependent on China (which controls ~60% of global REE production) for processed REE.

Why relevant now: Global efforts to reduce China-dependency (especially post-COVID and amid US-China trade wars) have elevated India's REE strategic importance. The government has launched Critical Minerals Mission to accelerate REE mining, processing, and supply chain development. REEs are essential for India's renewable energy (solar panels, wind turbines) and defense ambitions.
IIM Amritsar IIM Indore
What is AI's impact on education / the economy? What are AI's bad effects?
Structured Answer
Impact on Education: AI enables personalized learning, automated assessment, and access to quality education in remote areas. However, it raises concerns about academic dishonesty, over-dependence reducing critical thinking, and the digital divide (students without AI access being disadvantaged).

Bad Effects of AI (Broad): (1) Job displacement in routine cognitive tasks; (2) Deepfakes and misinformation; (3) Algorithmic bias and discrimination; (4) Privacy concerns from surveillance; (5) Concentration of AI power in few large companies; (6) Environmental cost of training large AI models.

India's CBSE introducing AI as a subject (referenced in IIM Indore interviews) is a positive policy step — integrating AI literacy into school curricula prepares students for an AI-augmented workforce.

IIM Amritsar IIM Indore
What is GIFT City? What are GDRs? What is MGNREGA? (Policy-linked GK)
Structured Answer
GIFT City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City): India's first International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) in Gandhinagar, Gujarat. It operates with a distinct regulatory regime to attract global financial services — similar to Singapore or Dubai DIFC. Houses NSE and BSE international exchanges.

GDRs (Global Depositary Receipts): Instruments issued by Indian companies to raise capital from international investors. A GDR represents ownership of shares in an Indian company, traded on foreign stock exchanges (like Luxembourg or London). Similar concept to ADRs (American Depositary Receipts).

MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act): Guarantees 100 days of wage employment per financial year to every rural household whose adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work. Benefits rural households (not all people — correct answer: rural people, not necessarily "low income" broadly).

Chess 960 (Fischer Random): A variant where the back row of pieces is randomly shuffled before the game — eliminating memorized opening theory and testing pure chess understanding.

Space & Aerospace

ISRO missions, NASA, ISS, Gaganyaan

IIM Amritsar IIM Amritsar
What is NASA? What is its connection with SpaceX? Who owns the ISS? What is the Gaganyaan mission?
Structured Answer
NASA — National Aeronautics and Space Administration — is the US space agency, founded in 1958. It is a government agency, not a private company.

NASA-SpaceX: SpaceX (Elon Musk's private company) has Commercial Crew contracts with NASA — it transports astronauts to the ISS via the Crew Dragon capsule, replacing the retired Space Shuttle and dependence on Russian Soyuz rockets.

ISS Ownership: The International Space Station is a collaborative project owned by USA (NASA), Russia (Roscosmos), Europe (ESA), Japan (JAXA), and Canada (CSA). No single country owns it exclusively.

Gaganyaan Mission: India's first human spaceflight program, targeting 3 crew members to a 400km LEO orbit for 3 days. Unmanned test missions (TV-D1, G1) have been conducted. The crewed mission is targeted for 2026-2027. Astronauts trained in Russia under the program.

What is India's renewable energy policy? What is the government doing in this sector?
Structured Answer
India's Renewable Energy Targets: India committed at COP26 to achieving 500 GW of non-fossil fuel electricity capacity by 2030, with 50% of energy from renewables. Currently at ~200 GW renewable capacity (solar: ~100 GW, wind: ~47 GW).

Key Initiatives: (1) PM Surya Ghar Yojana — rooftop solar for 1 crore households; (2) Green Hydrogen Mission — targeting 5 MMT production by 2030; (3) National Wind-Solar Hybrid Policy; (4) Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) support; (5) Viability Gap Funding for offshore wind; (6) PLI scheme for solar PV manufacturing.

The Adani Green Energy and Reliance New Energy are key private players. The government aims to make India a global hub for green hydrogen exports.
